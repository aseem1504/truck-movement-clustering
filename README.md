# Truck Movement Clustering

Group trucks by where they actually run, tag each one with the regions it covers, and find the workshops where trucks get serviced.

Built in 2020 for a truck GPS fleet. All trucks and pings in this repo are synthetic. Hub city coordinates are public.

## The problem

Truck owners describe their business in lanes: "Delhi to Mumbai", "local in Pune", "anything going east". But registration data says nothing about where a truck runs, and a GPS trail is millions of points. The business needed movement profiles for a few jobs:

- knowing which lanes the fleet really serves
- telling lenders which regions a financed truck works in
- placing installers and service partners where trucks actually stop

## How it works

**Movement similarity (two-layer DBSCAN).** Every truck has its top 10 most visited location clusters and the share of time spent in each. Turn that into a truck-by-cluster matrix of 0s and 1s. Run DBSCAN so trucks with nearly the same footprint group together. Then run DBSCAN again inside each group to split big lanes into sub-lanes.

**Region profile.** Add up each truck's time share by region. Tag it with every region above 20%. A Delhi-Mumbai truck becomes `NORTH + WEST`.

**Maintenance hubs (HDBSCAN).** Take the hour of slow pings before every "battery disconnected" event. Cluster them with HDBSCAN using real distances on the globe. Dense spots used by many trucks are workshops. These are also the places to watch for tampering.

**India grid.** A three-level grid over India's bounding box, so any ping can be bucketed fast at roughly 10 km, 50 km and 100 km scales.

## Results on synthetic data

`python demo.py` builds 1,200 trucks on 10 hidden lanes, plus pre-disconnect pings around 8 hidden workshops.

- Two-layer DBSCAN finds 18 groups and leaves 2% of trucks as noise. Agreement with the hidden lanes is 0.95 on the adjusted Rand index (1.0 is perfect). K-Means with the right number of lanes given to it scores 0.86.
- Most groups are pure. The one that is not mixes the eastern steel belt with the Delhi-Kolkata lane. The two share Dhanbad and Kolkata, so the trucks really do overlap.
- Region tags come out right for every lane. Delhi-Kolkata trucks read `NORTH + EAST`, Kolkata-Guwahati feeders read `EAST + NORTH_EAST`.
- All 8 workshops are found, each within 60 m, once you ask for at least 5 different trucks per hub.

## What the numbers say

DBSCAN beat K-Means without being told how many lanes exist. That matters, because nobody knows the real number of lanes in a fleet. It also leaves odd trucks as noise instead of forcing them into a group, which is honest. A truck that does random trips should not be sold as a Delhi-Mumbai regular.

The hub search needed one product rule that the maths could not supply: a hub has to be used by several different trucks. Without it, HDBSCAN returns 96 "hubs", most of them a single truck parked for a long time. Clustering finds dense spots. Deciding which dense spots matter is a business call.

## What changed from the original code

- Inputs are DataFrames instead of Mongo collections.
- HDBSCAN with the haversine metric needs `[lat, lon]` in radians. The original worked out radians but then fitted on raw `[lon, lat]` degrees. Fixed.
- Region tags are joined with `+` (`NORTH + EAST`), so they cannot be confused with the `NORTH_EAST` region.
- Hubs now need at least 5 distinct trucks (added).
- The grid builder is the original logic, packed into one function. The reverse-geocoding step, which needed an internal Mongo collection, is left in the notebook.

## Data and privacy

Trucks, lanes and pings are all generated by `simulate.py`. City coordinates are public. The original notebooks had their outputs, credentials, hosts and file paths removed before upload.
